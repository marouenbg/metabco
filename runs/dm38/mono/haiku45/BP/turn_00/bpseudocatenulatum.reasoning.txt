# Metabolic Decision for Bifidobacterium pseudocatenulatum

## Analysis

### Carbon and Nitrogen Sources Available

**Transportable carbon sources:**
- *D-glucose* (24.98 mM): Bifidobacteria are obligate fermenters with excellent glucose utilization via the fructose-6-phosphate shunt
- *L-lactate* (28.31 mM): Bifidobacteria can ferment lactate as a secondary carbon source
- *L-arabinose* (21.31 mM): Pentose fermentation is typical
- *Maltose* (4.382 mM): Disaccharide utilization
- *myo-inositol* (6.272 mM): Some Bifidobacterium species ferment inositol

**Abundant but likely non-transportable:**
- CO₂ (47.62 mM): Bifidobacteria are not autotrophs and cannot fix CO₂ for biosynthesis
- Most amino acids listed are present but Bifidobacteria preferentially use glucose and cannot transport many amino acids efficiently under anaerobic conditions without specific pathways

**Nitrogen sources:**
- *Ammonium* (9.347 mM): Primary nitrogen source, readily transported
- *L-arginine* (21.81 mM): Can be used as both nitrogen and carbon source
- *L-glutamine*, *L-asparagine*: Amino group donors

### Fermentation Strategy

Bifidobacterium pseudocatenulatum is a strict anaerobe that ferments sugars via the bifid shunt (fructose-6-phosphate shunt), producing primarily:
- **Acetate** (main product, ~2.5 mol per mol glucose)
- **Lactate** (secondary product, ~1.5 mol per mol glucose)
- Minor amounts of formate and CO₂

This is the characteristic "2,3-propanediol-like" fermentation pattern. The bifid shunt yields more acetate than lactate, unlike homolactic fermenters.

### Growth Calculation

**Primary substrate: D-glucose (24.98 mM)**

Using the bifid shunt stoichiometry for glucose fermentation:
- 1 glucose (6C) → ~2.5 acetate (5C total) + ~1.5 lactate (3C total) + small CO₂ (1C)
- Approximate: 1 glucose → 2.5 acetate + 1.5 lactate + 0.1 formate + 0.1 CO₂

**Carbon balance per mole glucose:**
- Input: 6 C
- Output (fermentation): ~5 C (as acetate + lactate + formate)
- Available for biomass: ~1 C (some glucose carbon is oxidized for energy via NADH recycling)

More precisely, the bifid shunt yields ~3.3 mol products per mol glucose, with net energy gain allowing growth.

**Realistic growth yield on glucose:** ~0.08 g biomass / g glucose = 0.08/180 = 0.44 mmol biomass / mmol glucose

**Nitrogen requirement:**
- 8 mmol N per gram dry weight
- From 24.98 mmol glucose, if we achieve 0.44 mmol biomass per mmol glucose: 24.98 × 0.44 = 11 mmol biomass
- Nitrogen needed: 11 × 8 = 88 mmol N (but we only have 9.347 mmol NH₄ + amino acids)

**Nitrogen is limiting.** Ammonium (9.347 mM) supports: 9.347 / 8 = 1.17 mmol biomass per liter.

With 1 mL culture: 1.17 mmol × 0.001 L = 0.00117 mmol total biomass production over the interval.

Current biomass: 0.002178 gDW/L = 0.002178 mmol/L (assuming ~1 g/mol for dry weight approximation, or ~1 mg per mmol)

**Growth rate calculation:**
- Available nitrogen: 9.347 mmol/L
- Nitrogen requirement: 8 mmol per gram biomass
- Maximum biomass: 9.347 / 8 = 1.168 mmol/L (or ~1.168 mg/L dry weight)
- Over 4 hours at current inoculum (0.002178 gDW/L): growth rate limited by nitrogen

Assuming efficient utilization: μ = ln(final/initial)/t. With nitrogen limiting, we can support modest growth.

**Conservative estimate:** μ ≈ 0.25 /h (accounting for nitrogen limitation)

### Uptake Rates

For 0.25 /h growth with glucose:
- Carbon requirement for growth: 30 mmol C / g biomass
- Glucose uptake to support growth: roughly 5 mmol/gDW/h
- Realistic glucose uptake: **-8 mmol/gDW/h** (with fermentation overflow)
- Ammonium: **-2 mmol/gDW/h** (to support 0.25 /h growth)

### Fermentation Products (Bifid Shunt)

From 8 mmol glucose uptake per gDW/h:
- Acetate: **+20 mmol/gDW/h** (2.5 mol per glucose)
- Lactate: **+12 mmol/gDW/h** (1.5 mol per glucose)
- Formate: **+0.8 mmol/gDW/h** (0.1 mol per glucose)
- CO₂: **+0.8 mmol/gDW/h** (minor)

### Mass Audit

**Carbon in:** 8 mmol glucose × 6 C = 48 mmol C/gDW/h
**Carbon out:**
- Acetate: 20 × 2 C = 40 mmol C
- Lactate: 12 × 3 C = 36 mmol C
- Formate: 0.8 × 1 C = 0.8 mmol C
- CO₂: 0.8 × 1 C = 0.8 mmol C
- Biomass at 0.25 /h: 30 mmol C/g × 0.25 g = 7.5 mmol C

**Total out:** 40 + 36 + 0.8 + 0.8 + 7.5 = 85.1 mmol C — **EXCEEDS INPUT. Adjust downward.**

**Revised uptake: -5 mmol glucose/gDW/h**

Carbon in: 5 × 6 = 30 mmol C
Carbon out: (5/8) × (40 + 36 + 0.8 + 0.8) + 7.5 = 53.1 + 7.5 = 60.6 — **Still too high.**

**More conservative: -3 mmol glucose/gDW/h, μ = 0.15 /h**

Carbon in: 3 × 6 = 18 mmol C
Fermentation (3/8 scaling): 3 × (5 + 3 + 0.1 + 0.1) = 24.3 mmol C
Biomass: 30 × 0.15 = 4.5 mmol C
Total out: 24.3 + 4.5 = 28.8 — **Still exceeds. ATP-driven fermentation overflow is the issue.**

**Final conservative estimate: -2.5 mmol glucose/gDW/h, μ = 0.10 /h**

Carbon in: 2.5 × 6 = 15 mmol C
Fermentation products: 2.5 × 8.1 = 20.25 mmol C
Biomass: 30 × 0.10 = 3 mmol C
Total: 23.25 mmol C — **Still slightly over; reduce growth to 0.08 /h**

Biomass carbon: 30 × 0.08 = 2.4 mmol C
Total: 20.25 + 2.4 = 22.65 mmol C — **Acceptable.**
