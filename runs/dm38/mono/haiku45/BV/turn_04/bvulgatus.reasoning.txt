# Metabolic reasoning for Bacteroides vulgatus at turn 4

## Assessment of the situation

I am at 2.388 gDW/L after 16 hours. The recent history shows I was growing well early (0.55–1.2 /h on glucose and lactate), but turns 2–3 show zero growth despite active fermentation. This suggests I exhausted my preferred carbon sources (glucose, arabinose, lactate are now gone or depleted) and entered a stationary/survival phase, catabolizing amino acids and releasing fermentation products without net biomass synthesis.

- **Acetate (ac)** 36.24 mM: I have acetyl-CoA synthase; can activate to acetyl-CoA
- **Butyrate (but)** 15.67 mM: can be activated via butyryl-CoA synthetase
- **Propionate (ppa)** 13.61 mM: can activate to propionyl-CoA
- **Short-chain fatty acids (SCFA)**: These are my fermentation products now accumulating; I can catabolize them via β-oxidation and the citric acid cycle (in anaerobic mode, glyoxylate shunt)
- **Amino acids**: L-arginine, L-cysteine, L-alanine, etc. — I can deaminate and ferment these; they are secondary carbon sources
- **Maltose** 4.38 mM: I likely have maltose PTS or symporter
- **myo-inositol** 6.27 mM: Bacteroides can ferment this
- **Lactate** 1.3 mM: still present in trace; can be oxidized

**Cannot readily use:**
- **CO₂** 74.11 mM: I am not autotrophic; CO₂ is a product, not a primary substrate

**Nitrogen sources:**
- **L-arginine** 8.785 mM: abundant amino acid; I can catabolize via arginine deiminase pathway (produces ammonia, citrulline, ATP)
- **L-cysteine** 8.4 mM: can be catabolized; yields sulfur and carbon
- **Other amino acids**: present but at lower levels
- **Ammonia (nh4)**: not listed as available, so likely depleted or very low

## Known fermentation strategy for B. vulgatus

B. vulgatus is a Gram-negative obligate anaerobe (Bacteroidetes). Its canonical fermentation products from carbohydrates and amino acids are:
- **Acetate** (major)
- **Butyrate** (major)
- **Propionate** (major)
- **CO₂**
- **H₂** (minor, if present)
- Trace formate, succinate

The accumulation of ac, but, and ppa in turns 2–3 confirms this. At pH 7 these are mostly in their anion forms and buffered.

## Current strategy: Amino acid catabolism

With glucose, lactate, and arabinose exhausted, I must now rely on:
1. **Amino acids** (arginine, cysteine, alanine, etc.) as carbon + nitrogen
2. **Accumulated SCFA** (acetate, butyrate, propionate) as alternative carbon (though these are harder to grow on anaerobically without carbohydrates; they support maintenance but slow growth)
3. **Maltose and inositol** as secondary carbohydrate sources

**Arginine catabolism** is energetically favorable:
- Arginine → citrulline + ammonia (via arginine deiminase, net ATP)
- Citrulline → carbamoyl phosphate + ornithine (regenerates ATP)
- Ornithine can be transaminated or further catabolized

This yields carbon skeletons (α-ketoglutarate family) and ammonia for biosynthesis.

## Growth rate calculation

Given:
- Biomass requirement: ~41 mmol C/gDW, ~8 mmol N/gDW
- Current biomass: 2.388 gDW/L
- Time interval: 4 h
- Available substrates: primarily amino acids + trace carbohydrates

**Limiting factors:**
- Nitrogen is limiting (amino acids are my only N source now)
- Arginine (8.8 mM) at 2.388 gDW/L means ~3.7 mmol N per gDW in the culture
- To grow 1 gDW, I need 8 mmol N, but I have only ~3.7 mmol N per gDW already present
- This suggests I can grow modestly on amino acids, but not at high rates

**Estimate:**
- If I take up arginine at ~2–3 mmol/gDW/h and couple it with trace maltose (~1 mmol/gDW/h), I can support ~0.15–0.25 /h growth
- I will also take up cysteine (N and S source) and alanine (C + N)
- Fermentation of these will yield acetate, butyrate, propionate, CO₂, and ammonia

**Conservative estimate: 0.18 /h growth rate**
