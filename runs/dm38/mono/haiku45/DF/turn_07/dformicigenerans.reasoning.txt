## Analysis of carbon and nitrogen sources

**Available transporters and substrates:**
- Dorea formicigenerans is a gram-positive anaerobic bacterium capable of fermenting carbohydrates and amino acids
- **Primary carbon sources present:** butyrate (49.88 mM), acetate (37.2 mM), formate (20.06 mM), CO₂ (99.16 mM)
- **Amino acids:** abundant L-serine (5.96 mM), L-proline (5.9 mM), L-alanine (5.3 mM), and others
- **Nitrogen:** amino acids provide nitrogen; nitrate (0.11 mM) is minimal and likely not preferred anaerobically

**Key constraint:** My recent history (turns 5–6) shows zero growth despite substrate availability. The pattern suggests I was consuming non-fermentable carbohydrates (arabinose, maltose, myo-inositol) that I cannot efficiently metabolize, and amino acids without net energy gain. I need to refocus on what I *can* ferment efficiently.

**What I can use:**
- **Butyrate and acetate:** These are short-chain fatty acids. As a butyrate-producing bacterium, I can oxidize butyrate via β-oxidation to acetyl-CoA, or use acetate directly. However, in a sealed anaerobic culture, these are typically fermentation products I *produce*, not consume efficiently for growth—they accumulate because I release them.
- **Amino acids:** Dorea can ferment amino acids (Stickland reactions, transamination, decarboxylation). L-serine, L-proline, and L-alanine are good candidates.
- **Formate and H₂:** Products of my own fermentation; limited use for further catabolism.
- **CO₂:** Primarily a product; limited uptake.

**Critical realization:** Turns 3–4 showed growth (0.65, 0.60 /h) when I was consuming L-arabinose and amino acids. Turn 5–6 showed zero growth when I shifted to non-fermentable carbohydrates. The arabinose and amino acids are now depleted or very low. I am in late stationary/decline phase.
