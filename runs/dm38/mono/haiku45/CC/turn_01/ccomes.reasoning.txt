# Reasoning through this metabolic decision

## What I can transport and metabolise

As *Coprococcus comes*, I am a Gram-positive anaerobic bacterium from the human gut microbiota. My genome encodes:
- **Carbohydrate transporters**: I can use glucose, arabinose, maltose (via PTS or permeases)
- **Organic acid transporters**: I can ferment lactate robustly — this is a signature trait
- **Amino acid transporters**: I have broad amino acid uptake capacity (arginine, serine, cysteine, etc.)
- **Limitations**: I cannot use nitrate (no nitrate reductase); I cannot fix nitrogen

## Carbon and nitrogen sources available

**Primary carbon sources I will use:**
- L-lactate (27.81 mM): abundant, preferred fermentation substrate
- D-glucose (24.65 mM): readily fermented
- L-arginine (21.81 mM): excellent dual C+N source; I can deaminate and ferment the carbon skeleton

**Amino acids as N sources:**
- Arginine is my best choice: it provides both carbon (from the α-ketoglutarate backbone after deamination) and nitrogen (from the guanidinium group)
- Ammonium (9.1 mM) is available but I prefer amino acids when abundant

**Why I avoid some substrates:**
- Butyrate production (not consumption) is my fermentation signature
- I cannot efficiently use nitrate in anaerobic conditions

## Fermentation strategy

From my last turn, I was growing at 0.700 /h. I should sustain or slightly increase this given abundant substrates remain.

**My fermentation products:**
- Butyrate (major): from lactate and glucose fermentation via the butyryl-CoA pathway
- Acetate (co-product): from mixed-acid fermentation
- CO₂: from decarboxylation reactions
- Small amounts of formate or succinate may occur

## Growth rate calculation

**Substrate uptake plan:**
- Lactate: 10 mmol/gDW/h (robust fermentation)
- Glucose: 6 mmol/gDW/h (secondary, spare capacity)
- Arginine: 4 mmol/gDW/h (for nitrogen + supplemental C)
- Ammonium: 1 mmol/gDW/h (fill remaining N demand)

**Carbon balance:**
- Lactate C: 10 × 3 = 30 mmol C
- Glucose C: 6 × 6 = 36 mmol C
- Arginine C: 4 × 6 = 24 mmol C
- **Total C in: 90 mmol/gDW**

**Nitrogen balance:**
- Arginine N: 4 × 4 = 16 mmol N
- Ammonium N: 1 mmol N
- **Total N in: 17 mmol/gDW**

**Growth cost:**
- C cost: 30 mmol/gDW per gram biomass produced
- N cost: 8 mmol/gDW per gram biomass produced
- Max growth from C: 90 / 30 = 3.0 gDW/gDW (nonsensical; this is per hour rate)
- Max growth from N: 17 / 8 = 2.125 /h (also high; N is not limiting)

**Realistic growth rate:** Given substrate abundance and my metabolic capacity, **μ = 0.65 /h** is sustainable (slightly below the prior 0.700 to account for substrate depletion over 4 h).

**Carbon accounting for growth at 0.65 /h:**
- Biomass produced: 0.65 gDW/gDW/h
- C for growth: 0.65 × 30 = 19.5 mmol C
- C for fermentation products: 90 − 19.5 = 70.5 mmol C
- Fermentation yields: lactate + glucose → butyrate + acetate + CO₂
  - Lactate (3C) → typically 1.5 acetate (2C) + 0.5 butyrate (4C) + CO₂
  - Glucose (6C) → 2 acetate + butyrate + CO₂ (various ratios)
  - Approximate: 70.5 mmol C → ~20 mmol butyrate (80 C) + ~25 mmol acetate (50 C) + ~15 mmol CO₂

This is consistent with observed fermentation patterns.

## Nitrogen audit
- N in: 17 mmol
- N for growth at 0.65 /h: 0.65 × 8 = 5.2 mmol
- N excess: 11.8 mmol (will accumulate as amino groups, released as NH₄ or incorporated into overflow amino acids)
