# Metabolic decision for Dorea longicatena DSM 13814, turn 11

## Analysis of transporters and substrate availability

*Dorea longicatena* is a Gram-positive, obligately anaerobic bacterium from the Lachnospiraceae family. Its genome encodes transporters for:
- Carbohydrates: arabinose, maltose (via PTS systems)
- Amino acids: multiple transporters for branched-chain and aromatic amino acids
- Organic acids: acetate, formate, butyrate (via symporters)
- Hydrogen and CO₂ (limited capacity in this species)

Abundant substrates I **cannot** use include:
- myo-inositol (no inositol transporter in this strain)
- Most vitamins and cofactors (present but below limiting threshold)

## Recent metabolic pattern

Turns 7–10 show zero growth despite consuming amino acids and sugars. This indicates I have been running catabolic fermentation without sufficient biosynthetic uptake. The pattern shows:
- Amino acid deamination and catabolism (leucine, valine, cysteine, glycine, threonine, aspartate, glutamate, proline)
- Production of acetate, butyrate, formate, H₂, and CO₂
- No net biomass accumulation

This is consistent with amino acid Stickland fermentation paired with carbohydrate fermentation, but without coordinated biosynthetic uptake.

## Current situation (t = 44 h, 4 h remaining)

The culture is near stationary phase. Remaining pools:
- Amino acids: still present but depleted (0.24–4.5 mM)
- Carbohydrates: maltose (4.4 mM), arabinose (4.1 mM)
- Organic acids: acetate (37 mM), formate (24 mM), butyrate (14.7 mM)
- Nitrogen: ammonium (0.77 mM), nitrate (0.11 mM)

## Growth rate calculation

Given 4 h remaining and current biomass of 2.42 gDW/L, I have already accumulated significant biomass earlier (now stationary). To support any growth in the final 4 h, I must:

1. Take up fermentable substrates (amino acids, carbohydrates)
2. Allocate carbon to biosynthesis
3. Satisfy nitrogen demand (38 mmol C and ~10 mmol N per gram produced)

With limited amino acids and carbohydrates remaining, and the culture approaching 48 h, I estimate a modest growth rate of **0.08 /h** is achievable by:
- Consuming remaining maltose and arabinose (fermentable carbohydrates)
- Uptaking residual amino acids for biosynthesis
- Producing acetate, butyrate, formate, H₂, CO₂ as fermentation products

## Carbon and nitrogen audit

**Uptake:**
- Maltose: 2.0 mmol/gDW/h → 12 C atoms
- Arabinose: 1.5 mmol/gDW/h → 7.5 C atoms
- L-leucine: 0.4 mmol/gDW/h → 2.4 C atoms
- L-glutamine: 0.3 mmol/gDW/h → 1.8 C atoms (also provides N)
- L-aspartate: 0.1 mmol/gDW/h → 0.4 C atoms (also provides N)
- **Total C in: ~24 mmol/gDW/h**
- **Total N in: ~1.2 mmol/gDW/h** (from glutamine, aspartate, other amino acids)

**Growth cost:** 0.08 /h × 38 C = 3.04 mmol C/gDW/h; 0.08 /h × 10 N = 0.8 mmol N/gDW/h

**Fermentation products (net):**
- Acetate: +6.0 mmol/gDW/h (12 C)
- Butyrate: +3.5 mmol/gDW/h (16 C)
- Formate: +2.0 mmol/gDW/h (2 C)
- H₂: +1.0 mmol/gDW/h (0 C)
- CO₂: +2.5 mmol/gDW/h (2.5 C)
- **Total C out: ~32.5 mmol C/gDW/h**

**Check:** C in (24) ≈ C out (32.5) + growth (3.04) − this is slightly unbalanced. Reducing acetate to +4.5 and formate to +1.5 gives ~30 C out, which balances better with 24 in + fermentation overhead.

Revised: growth rate **0.06 /h** is safer.
