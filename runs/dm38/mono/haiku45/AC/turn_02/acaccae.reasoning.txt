# Metabolic decision for Anaerostipes caccae DSM 14662, turn 2

## Analysis of transporters and substrate availability

Anaerostipes caccae is a strict anaerobe, butyrate-producing bacterium. Based on genomic and phenotypic data, this species can ferment:
- **Sugars**: glucose, arabinose, maltose (hexoses and pentoses via PTS and other transporters)
- **Organic acids**: lactate (via lactate permease)
- **Amino acids**: arginine (via arginine deiminase pathway), serine, other amino acids for catabolism and biosynthesis

The abundant substrates I *cannot* efficiently use include:
- **Inositol, proline, phenylalanine, tyrosine, valine, leucine, isoleucine**: These require specific transporters not well-characterized in this strain, or their catabolism is energetically unfavorable in this context.
- **Cysteine**: While present, it is limited and better reserved for biosynthesis (methionine/cysteine metabolism).

## Carbon and nitrogen sources I will use

**Primary carbon sources:**
- **D-glucose** (22.49 mM): Excellent substrate, high uptake rate expected
- **L-lactate** (27.88 mM): Secondary carbon source, supports growth
- **L-arabinose** (21.2 mM): Fermentable pentose
- **L-arginine** (21.36 mM): Nitrogen and carbon source via arginine deiminase

**Nitrogen sources:**
- **Ammonium** (9.223 mM): Primary inorganic nitrogen
- **L-arginine**: Dual role (carbon + nitrogen)
- **L-serine, L-alanine**: Minor amino acid nitrogen

## Fermentation strategy

A. caccae is known to produce **butyrate** as a major end product, along with **acetate**, **formate**, and **CO₂**. Previous turns show this pattern clearly.

## Growth calculation

From turn 1: growth was 0.450 /h with glucose uptake of 14 mmol/gDW/h.

At turn 2, I have depleted some glucose (was ~22.49 mM remaining) and lactate is abundant. I will continue mixed-substrate fermentation:

**Proposed uptake:**
- Glucose: 12 mmol/gDW/h (slightly reduced as it is becoming limiting)
- Lactate: 4 mmol/gDW/h (supplementary carbon)
- Arabinose: 3 mmol/gDW/h
- Arginine: 2 mmol/gDW/h (nitrogen + carbon)
- Serine: 1 mmol/gDW/h (minor amino acid)
- Ammonium: 3 mmol/gDW/h (primary nitrogen)

**Total carbon uptake:** 12 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 22 mmol C/gDW/h

**Nitrogen uptake:** 3 (from NH₄) + 2 (from arg, ~1 N per arg) + 0.5 (from ser) = 5.5 mmol N/gDW/h

**Growth requirement:** 38 mmol C and 10 mmol N per gram of biomass.

With 22 mmol C available and 5.5 mmol N available:
- Carbon permits: 22 / 38 = 0.58 g biomass
- Nitrogen permits: 5.5 / 10 = 0.55 g biomass

**Limiting factor: nitrogen** → growth rate ≈ **0.40 /h** (conservative, accounting for maintenance)

## Fermentation products (mass balance)

Carbon balance:
- In: 22 mmol C/gDW/h
- Biomass cost: 0.40 × 38 = 15.2 mmol C/gDW/h
- Available for fermentation: 22 - 15.2 = 6.8 mmol C/gDW/h

Products (typical A. caccae fermentation, 60% butyrate, 30% acetate, 10% formate/CO₂):
- **Butyrate**: 6.8 × 0.60 / 4 = 1.02 mmol/gDW/h
- **Acetate**: 6.8 × 0.30 / 2 = 1.02 mmol/gDW/h
- **Formate + CO₂**: 6.8 × 0.10 = 0.68 mmol/gDW/h (split as formate 0.34, CO₂ 0.34)

## Confidence and rationale

This strategy reflects known A. caccae metabolism: mixed-acid fermentation with butyrate as the dominant product. Nitrogen becomes limiting, which is realistic in batch culture. Growth rate is moderate and sustainable given substrate depletion over 8 hours.
