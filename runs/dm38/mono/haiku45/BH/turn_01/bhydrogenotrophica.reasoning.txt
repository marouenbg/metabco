# Metabolic decision for Blautia hydrogenotrophica DSM 10507, turn 1

## Analysis of available transporters and carbon sources

Blautia hydrogenotrophica is a strict anaerobe and obligate hydrogen-oxidizing bacterium. Its core metabolic strategy is **hydrogenotrophic autotrophy** — it oxidizes H₂ and reduces CO₂ to acetate. However, this organism also has the capacity to ferment sugars and organic acids when available, which is ecologically important in the human gut.

**Carbon sources I can transport:**
- **D-glucose (glc__D)**: Yes — Blautia has glucose transporters and can ferment glucose via glycolysis
- **L-lactate (lac__L)**: Yes — lactate is a known substrate; can be oxidized or fermented
- **L-arabinose (arab__L)**: Yes — pentose fermentation is typical for gut Firmicutes
- **Maltose (malt)**: Yes — disaccharide fermentation
- **Amino acids (arg__L, cys__L, ser__L, etc.)**: Yes — amino acid fermentation is a hallmark of this genus; Blautia uses the Stickland reaction and amino acid deamination
- **CO₂**: Yes — as terminal electron acceptor in hydrogenotrophy (if H₂ is available)
- **H₂**: Yes — the primary energy substrate for this species

**Carbon sources I likely cannot access efficiently:**
- **myo-inositol (inost)**: Requires specific inositol transporters; uncertain
- **4-Aminobenzoate, xanthine, orotate**: Unlikely given genomic profile

## Fermentation products

Blautia hydrogenotrophica is known to produce:
- **Acetate** (primary product from both H₂/CO₂ and sugar fermentation)
- **Formate** (from glucose fermentation)
- **H₂** (from lactate and amino acid fermentation)
- **CO₂** (from decarboxylation reactions)

The organism can also produce small amounts of butyrate or propionate under certain conditions, but acetate dominates.
